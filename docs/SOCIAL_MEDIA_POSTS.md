```
Where does a student's money actually go? I built a Python project to find out. 💸

I created a Personal Expense Tracker with Data Visualization. It takes raw, messy expense records and turns them into clean data, budget alerts, 6 charts, and an auto-generated financial report, all from one command.

🔍 What it does
• Cleans messy data: duplicates, "N/A" amounts, negative values, inconsistent category names, invalid dates (382 raw rows → 364 clean)
• Analyses spending by category, month, payment method and day
• Compares every month against a category budget to flag overspending
• Calculates the savings rate and the average daily spend
• Generates 6 charts plus a text report and CSV summaries automatically

📊 Insights from 6 months of simulated student data
• Rent is the biggest expense (25%), but Food & Dining (16%) is the biggest one you can actually control
• Weekends cost 60% more per day than weekdays
• Festival and trip months (March, May) pushed spending above income
• Groceries went over budget in 5 of 6 months
• Average savings rate: 11.7%

🛠️ Tech stack: Python | Pandas | NumPy | Matplotlib | Seaborn | Streamlit (optional dashboard)

💡 What I learned
1. Real-world data is messy, and cleaning is most of the work
2. groupby, pivot_table and rolling averages answer most business questions
3. The right chart matters: bars for comparison, lines for trends
4. Numbers become valuable only when you turn them into a decision

Since I don't have access to real banking data, I wrote a generator that simulates realistic transactions with weekend effects, seasonality and deliberate data errors. That way the cleaning step faces real-world problems.

🔗 Code, charts and documentation: https://github.com/SNischayPrasad/Personal-Expense-Tracker-Visualization

I'd love feedback from data and finance folks. What feature should I add next: automatic categorisation with ML, or a spending forecast?
```
